\section{Background and Related Work}
Pretrained language models have long been probed as implicit knowledge bases, a
line that \citet{petroni2019lama} opened by casting factual recall as cloze
probing. Subsequent work sharpened the prompts \citep{jiang2020lpaqa,
shin2020autoprompt,zhong2021optiprompt}, quantified how much knowledge a model's
parameters hold \citep{roberts2020closedbook}, and questioned how reliably that
knowledge can be read out \citep{cao2021knowledgeable,alkhamissi2022review}.
These probes share a common assumption: a single gold object per query, scored
by how highly the model ranks it. We instead target the set-valued,
alias-graded variant in which the empty set is a valid answer, a setting far
closer to materialising an actual knowledge base than to ranking a single token
\citep{pan2023llmkg}.

A parallel thread turns language models toward building knowledge bases rather
than merely probing them, and the LM-KBC shared task is its clearest instance.
Across its editions \citep{alivanistos2022prompting,kalo2023lmkbc,kalo2024lmkbc,
kalo2025lmkbc} the task has moved from masked-LM probing and prompt ensembling
toward instruction-tuned generative systems, with some earlier tracks permitting
retrieval or fine-tuning \citep{zhang2023llmke}. The 2026 edition removes those
options with its closed-book, no-tuning, $\le$32B rules, and it is exactly this
stripped-down regime that forces the parametric-recall-with-abstention design we
pursue.

The usual route to knowledge-intensive accuracy under such pressure is retrieval
augmentation \citep{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm}, which helps most precisely when
parametric memory is weak \citep{mallen2023whennot}. Self-RAG
\citep{asai2024selfrag} pushes this further by letting the model itself decide
\emph{when} to retrieve. We borrow the spirit of a model-driven, adaptive
decision, but because retrieval is forbidden we redirect it to a different
question: not what to fetch, but \emph{when to answer at all}.

That decision draws together three further threads that converge in our method.
Self-consistency \citep{wang2022selfconsistency,chen2023universalsc} samples a
model repeatedly and aggregates the generations to select a single answer;
abstaining when uncertain is the classic reject option
\citep{geifman2017selective}, studied in NLP as calibration
\citep{guo2017calibration}, unanswerable QA \citep{rajpurkar2018squad2}, and
selective QA \citep{cole2023selective}, and language models retain some awareness
of what they know \citep{kadavath2022know}. We combine these: cross-sample
\emph{disagreement} \citep{kuhn2023semantic,manakul2023selfcheckgpt} serves as our
zero-parameter confidence signal, and, unlike majority-vote self-consistency,
which always emits an answer, we let that signal trigger \emph{abstention}. Our
setting differs from all of the above in one decisive way, and it is what makes
abstention necessary rather than optional: the objects form a set of unbounded
cardinality, the prediction is closed-book and untuned, and the empty set is
itself a valid, high-value prediction rather than a failure to be avoided.
