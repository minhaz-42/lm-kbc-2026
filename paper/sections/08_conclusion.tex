\section{Conclusion}
We treated \emph{when to answer} as the central decision in closed-book,
set-valued KB construction, and derived each component from one metric fact:
because macro-F\textsubscript{1} rewards a correct empty set, abstention pays off,
numeric recall is scarce, and scope grounding curbs over- and under-prediction.
Instantiated with Gemma-4-31B, the system reaches $0.459$/$0.490$
(relation-averaged/micro) and beats the official baseline on all six relations,
reproducibly from a cached pass. The durable lesson is comparative: the 30B wins
with single-sample decoding, while abstention and self-consistency chiefly rescue
weaker models like the 14B, so at this budget model scale dominates decode-time
voting even though the two remain complementary levers. Pairing the voting with the
30B, which API limits blocked here, is the clearest next step.
