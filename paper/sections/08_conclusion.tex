\section{Conclusion}
We treated \emph{when to answer} as the central decision in closed-book,
set-valued KB construction, deriving every component from one metric fact: because
macro-F\textsubscript{1} rewards a correct empty set, abstention pays off, numeric
recall is scarce, and scope grounding curbs over- and under-prediction. Instantiated with Gemma-4-31B, the system reaches $0.459$
relation-averaged macro-F\textsubscript{1} ($0.490$ micro), beats the official
baseline on all six relations, and is reproducible from a cached generation pass;
a smaller 4-bit Qwen2.5-14B reaches $0.416$. The durable lesson is comparative:
the 30B wins with single-sample decoding, while
abstention and self-consistency chiefly rescue the smaller and weaker models. At
this budget model scale dominates the decode-time voting, but the two are
complementary levers whose balance shifts as models strengthen. Pairing this
machinery with the 30B, which API limits blocked here, is the clearest next step.
