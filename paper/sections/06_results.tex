\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
Our primary system, \textbf{Gemma-4-31B}, scores \textbf{0.461}
relation-averaged macro-F\textsubscript{1} (\textbf{0.488} micro) on validation,
versus $0.169$/$0.195$ for the empty baseline, $0.300$/$0.313$ for the official
baseline, and $0.416$/$0.442$ for the smaller Qwen2.5-14B. Figure~\ref{fig:bars}
and Table~\ref{tab:results} give the per-relation picture. Gemma-4-31B beats the
official baseline on \emph{five of the six} relations, the exception being
\textsc{hasCapacity} ($0.17$ vs.\ $0.18$, within decoding noise). Over the 14B, its
gains concentrate where the model already has usable knowledge: the biggest
14B\,$\to$\,30B jumps are on \textsc{companyTradesAtStockExchange}
($0.61\!\to\!0.74$) and \textsc{hasCapacity} ($0.09\!\to\!0.17$), while the
near-saturated \textsc{countryLandBordersCountry} reaches $0.971$. It ties the 14B
on \textsc{hasArea} and trails it on \textsc{personHasCityOfDeath} ($0.42$ vs.\
$0.45$), the relation where the single-sample 30B forgoes the abstention voting the
14B uses. Strikingly, the 30B attains this overall lead \emph{without}
self-consistency or abstention, from a single sample. We could not run voting on
the 30B, so we isolate the decode-time levers on the smaller models in
Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
