\section*{Limitations}
\paragraph{Held-out evaluation.}
We report validation results only; no test-set numbers are available yet. Our
reported systems use a fixed, a-priori $\theta{=}0.5$, but the diagnostic threshold
sweep (Figure~\ref{fig:ablate}) is run on the same split it is scored on, so its
per-relation optima are optimistic rather than held-out and should be read as
diagnostic only.

\paragraph{Decoding variance.}
We measure run-to-run variance for the 14B only (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}), by
scoring each cached draw separately; this covers five of the six relations, since
\textsc{awardWonBy} was decoded once. The estimate uses three to five runs, so the
per-relation standard deviations are themselves noisy, and \textsc{awardWonBy} has
only 10 subjects. Because the 30B is single-sample we cannot measure its variance
directly and assume it is comparable. Small per-relation deltas should accordingly
be read as indicative only.

\paragraph{Model coverage.}
Being closed-book, the system inherits the base model's coverage and recency
limits and cannot recover facts the model never stored. This caps
\textsc{awardWonBy} recall and rare-venue numeric accuracy, and it means our gains
reflect parametric recall rather than any external grounding.

\paragraph{Single-sample 30B.}
Because API rate limits precluded multi-sample decoding, our Gemma-4-31B scores are
single-sample. We therefore cannot report how the 30B behaves under the
self-consistency and abstention voting that help the smaller models; pairing them
is left to future work and may further lift the null-heavy and numeric relations,
so our 30B numbers are likely a floor rather than a ceiling.

\paragraph{Metric specificity.}
The abstention advantage stems from this task's macro metric and its
empty-as-valid-answer setting. It may not transfer to single-answer or micro-only
regimes, and practitioners porting the method to other benchmarks should re-verify
that abstention is still a positive-value action before adopting the voting
machinery.
