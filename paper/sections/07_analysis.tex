\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
Because the headline 30B result is single-sample, we isolate the decode-time
levers on the 14B and 0.5B models, which we can sample freely. All ablations below
are decoded from the same cached samples, and matter most where the metric is
unforgiving: the null-heavy and high-cardinality relations.

\paragraph{Abstention.}
Abstention rescues weak models and refines strong ones.
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates it on the three null-heavy relations. On a
poorly-calibrated 0.5B model the effect is dramatic ($0.173\!\to\!0.372$): without
voting it over-answers and falls \emph{below} the empty baseline ($0.350$). The
14B is already above that baseline without voting ($0.652$), so voting adds less
($0.652\!\to\!0.666$) but still helps. Abstention buys precision: on
\textsc{personHasCityOfDeath} it moves $P{=}0.77\!\to\!0.81\!\to\!0.89$ as $\theta$
rises, trading a little recall. It suppresses wrong guesses, not correct answers.

\paragraph{Setting the threshold.}
If abstention drives the string relations, the natural next question is how to set
its threshold, and here the empty rate is the guide while numeric voting stays
neutral. Figure~\ref{fig:ablate} (left) sweeps $\theta$: the most null-heavy
relation, \textsc{personHasCityOfDeath} (45\% empty), peaks at a \emph{lower}
$\theta$, while the others tolerate aggressive voting, arguing for per-relation
thresholds. Figure~\ref{fig:ablate} (right) varies the number of numeric samples:
median aggregation is non-monotonic on \textsc{hasArea} and actually slightly
\emph{hurts} \textsc{hasCapacity} (single sample $0.10$ vs.\ median-5 $0.09$).
Decode variance is not the bottleneck; the gap is knowledge-bound. The model's
capacity guesses are the right order of magnitude but fall outside the 5\% band
(e.g.\ Spartan Stadium: predicted $37{,}000$, gold $75{,}005$).

\paragraph{The \textsc{awardWonBy} recall cap.}
The relation \textsc{awardWonBy}, which we beat the baseline on only narrowly, is
recall-capped, and this limit constrains both models. Gold sets average
${\sim}148$ recipients (max 641), but in the 14B ablation the model enumerates
only ${\sim}11$ names; its outputs are \emph{not} truncated, it closes the JSON
early, capping recall near $11/148\approx0.07$. The listed names are often
plausible but wrong ($P{=}0.36$). Parametric recall and a reluctance to enumerate
bound this relation, so a larger in-budget model or multi-step decomposition would
help most.

\paragraph{Qualitative behaviour.}
Table~\ref{tab:qual} shows representative Gemma-4-31B outputs. The model is exact
on well-known facts (\textsc{personHasCityOfDeath}, \textsc{hasCapacity}) and
near-complete on borders, but the two error modes are visible: a numeric near-miss
on \textsc{hasArea}, and on \textsc{awardWonBy} a list of globally famous names
rather than the actual (obscurer) Stockholm honorary doctors, plausible but wrong,
the recall/hallucination limit quantified above.
