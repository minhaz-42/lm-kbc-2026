\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
Because the headline 30B result is single-sample, we isolate the decode-time levers
on the 14B, which we can sample freely; all ablations use the same cached samples.

\paragraph{Run-to-run variance.}
Decoding is stochastic ($\tau{=}0.7$), so no margin should be read before knowing
how far a score moves between runs. The cached 14B draws are independent, so
scoring the pipeline on each draw \emph{separately} yields repeated single-sample
runs at no extra cost. Across them the relation-averaged
macro-F\textsubscript{1} is $0.404\pm0.006$ (micro $0.428\pm0.008$; sd over five
runs), but the per-relation spread is very uneven:
\textsc{countryLandBordersCountry} is nearly deterministic (sd $0.002$), whereas
\textsc{hasCapacity} ranges over $0.04$--$0.11$ (sd $0.030$) and
\textsc{personHasCityOfDeath} over $0.41$--$0.44$ (sd $0.017$). Small per-relation
gaps in Table~\ref{tab:results} are therefore not interpretable: the $0.03$ by
which the 14B leads the 30B on \textsc{personHasCityOfDeath} is inside that
relation's own run-to-run range, as is the $0.01$ separating the 30B from the
official baseline on \textsc{hasCapacity}. The aggregate ordering, by contrast, is
stable: the $0.057$ by which the 30B leads is roughly nine times the aggregate sd.

\paragraph{Backbone or decoding?}
Our headline comparison could conflate two factors, since the 30B is decoded with a
single sample and the 14B with the full aggregation. Holding decoding fixed
separates them. At \emph{matched} single-sample decoding ($N{=}1$, no voting, no
median) the 30B scores $0.461$ and the 14B $0.404\pm0.006$, a $+0.057$ backbone
effect; switching the 14B's full decode-time machinery on moves it from $0.404$ to
$0.415$, a $+0.011$ decoding effect. The backbone gap is about five times the
largest decoding gain available on the 14B, so the model-scale conclusion does not
rest on the two systems having been decoded differently. It also bounds what voting
could plausibly add to the 30B: were it to gain what the 14B gains, it would reach
roughly $0.47$, which would widen rather than overturn the ordering.

\paragraph{Abstention.}
Abstention matters most exactly where the metric rewards silence. On the three
null-heavy relations, even predicting nothing beats the official 9B baseline
(Section~\ref{sec:task}), so the question is whether a real model does better by
abstaining \emph{selectively} rather than never. It does: decoding the 14B with
consistency voting lifts its null-heavy average from $0.642$ (single sample) to
$0.659$ at $\theta{=}0.5$. The gain is modest but consistent, and it comes from
precision: raising $\theta$ collapses inconsistent (usually wrong) guesses to
$\emptyset$, trading a little recall to suppress false positives.

\paragraph{Setting the threshold.}
How high should $\theta$ be? Figure~\ref{fig:ablate} sweeps it for the 14B on the
null-heavy relations. Our deployed systems fix $\theta{=}0.5$; the sweep is
diagnostic (run on the split we report on, so its optima are optimistic, not tuned
back in). Even so, the pattern argues for per-relation thresholds:
\textsc{personHasCityOfDeath} (39\% empty) peaks at a low $\theta$ and falls off as
voting grows stricter, while \textsc{countryLandBordersCountry} and
\textsc{companyTradesAtStockExchange} tolerate aggressive voting. Numerics differ:
median aggregation is roughly neutral, so their gap is knowledge- rather than
variance-bound, with area and capacity guesses often the right order of magnitude
but outside the 5\% band (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-qual}).

\paragraph{The \textsc{awardWonBy} recall cap.}
\textsc{awardWonBy}, which we beat the baseline on only narrowly, is recall-capped
for both models. Gold sets average ${\sim}146$ recipients (max 610), but the 14B
enumerates only ${\sim}11$ names: its outputs are \emph{not} truncated, it closes
the JSON early, capping recall near $11/146\approx0.07$, and the names it does list
are often plausible but wrong ($P{=}0.39$). Within a closed-book single model this
relation is largely unsolved; a larger in-budget model or multi-step decomposition
would help most.
